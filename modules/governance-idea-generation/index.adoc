== Learning outcomes

* [[lo-1]] Explain why idea generation and idea selection are separated

* [[lo-3]] Name two further techniques and say what situation each suits

== Diverge first, converge later

Two activities, opposite rules, and the classic mistake is doing both at once:

[cols="1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Diverging |Converging

|as many ideas as possible
|as few as necessary

|no evaluation, no "that won't work"
|evaluation is the whole point

|quantity, variety, building on others
|criteria, weights, a decision

|this module
|next module — weighted scoring
|===

The moment somebody says "that's not feasible" during the diverging phase, the
group stops producing ideas and starts defending them. That is why the phases
are separated in time, not just in intention.

== 6-3-5 brainwriting

Six people, three ideas each, five minutes per round, six rounds. Every round the
sheet moves one seat on, and you build on what is already written.

[plantuml,brainwriting,svg]
----
@startuml
skinparam monochrome true
skinparam shadowing false
start
:each person gets a sheet with 3 columns;
repeat
  :write 3 ideas in 5 minutes;
  :pass the sheet to the next person;
  :read what is there, build on it;
repeat while (six rounds done?) is (no)
->yes;
:6 x 3 x 6 = 108 written ideas;
stop
@enduml
----

Why this one and not a group discussion:

* *Everyone contributes.* In a discussion two people talk and four agree.
* *The result is written.* No transcription step, no "what did we say?", and the
  sheets are raw material for the project proposals directly.
* *It is time-boxed by construction.* Five minutes per round, and the round ends
  because the sheet moves, not because somebody decides it does.
* *Building on others is forced.* You read three ideas before writing your own.

The output is not 108 good ideas. It is maybe five worth pursuing, and the other
hundred are the price of finding them.

== Two more techniques worth knowing

[cols="1,2",options="header"]
|===
|Technique |Suits

|*Mind mapping*
|one person or a pair, exploring an area where the structure is still unclear;
 fast, visual, but the loudest branch tends to win

|*SCAMPER* (substitute, combine, adapt, modify, put to other use, eliminate,
 reverse)
|a stuck group with an existing solution — it forces variation on something
 concrete instead of asking for inspiration from nothing
|===

== Decisions

* 6-3-5 is the one technique actually carried out in this course; the others are
  named and placed, not practised.
* Idea generation happens in the theory unit, without machines. It needs paper,
  not an IDE.
* The sheets are kept. They become the raw material for the project proposals in
  the following lessons.

== Pitfalls

* Evaluating during the diverging phase. It ends the phase.
* Skipping the reading step and just writing your own three ideas again. The
  method then degenerates into six people writing alone.
* Asking an agent for "50 project ideas" and calling it done. You get plausible
  averages, not ideas anybody in the room cares about — and caring is what gets a
  project through February.
