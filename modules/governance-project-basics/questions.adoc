== Which criteria make a piece of work a project? Give an example that fails one

covers: lo-1

.Answer
[%collapsible]
====
A project is *unique*, *time-bounded*, *resourced* and *goal-directed*, and it is
complex enough that it has to be organised. All five together — a single one is
not enough.

[cols="2,1,2",options="header"]
|===
|Work |Project? |Which criterion decides

|Your year project
|yes
|unique goal, fixed end, a team, a deliverable nobody has built yet

|Answering support mail
|no
|not unique and not time-bounded — continuous operation

|Migrating the school website to a new host
|yes
|one-off, dated, resourced, with a result that can be accepted or rejected

|Weekly backups
|no
|repeats; there is no end and no unique result

|Fixing a printer
|no
|routine, and not complex enough to need organising
|===

The distinction is not academic. If it is a project, somebody must be able to say
afterwards whether it *succeeded* — and that is only possible if a goal was
written down before the work started. Routine work is judged differently: it is
judged by whether it keeps running.

Points the answer must contain:

* Unique, time-bounded, resourced, goal-directed, complex enough to organise
* Counter-example: routine work such as support mail — continuous, repeating,
  no unique result
====

== What belongs in an initial situation, and what does not?

covers: lo-2

.Answer
[%collapsible]
====
The *Ausgangslage* describes what exists today and what is unsatisfactory about
it: who has the problem, what happens now, and what it costs in time, money or
nerves. It does not describe your solution and it names no technology.

[cols="1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Not an initial situation |An initial situation

|"We build an app with Spring Boot and a React frontend."
|"The club's attendance list is kept on paper. Trainers copy it into a
 spreadsheet after training, which takes about twenty minutes a week and is
 often forgotten."

|"There is no digital solution yet."
|"Members cannot see whether a session is full before they arrive, so about a
 quarter of them travel for nothing."
|===

The test: *could somebody who has never heard of your project recognise the
problem?* If the text only makes sense once your solution is known, it is a
solution description wearing the wrong hat.

The consequence of getting this wrong is not stylistic. If the solution is
written into the problem, then the question "was this the right solution?" can
never be answered — the yardstick and the thing being measured are the same
object. And "there is no app yet" is not a problem; nobody suffers from the
absence of an app, they suffer from twenty minutes a week and from wasted trips.

Points the answer must contain:

* Who has the problem, what happens today, what it costs
* Not the solution, not the technology
* Test: a reader who does not know your solution must still recognise the problem
====

== Name the three constraints and explain which one moves in a school year

covers: lo-3

.Answer
[%collapsible]
====
*Scope* (what is built), *time* (when it is due) and *resources* (people, money,
machines). They are connected: changing one forces a change in at least one of
the others.

.Two corners are nailed down by the school year
[graphviz,q-triangle,svg]
----
digraph triangle {
  bgcolor="transparent";
  layout=neato;
  node [shape=box, style=filled, fillcolor="#f2f2f2", color="#666666",
        fontname="Helvetica", fontsize=11];
  edge [color="#666666", arrowhead=none];

  S [label="SCOPE\nwhat is built\n— the only variable", pos="0,1.4!",
     fillcolor="#ffffff"];
  T [label="TIME\nthe year ends\n— fixed", pos="-1.6,-0.6!"];
  R [label="RESOURCES\nthe team you are\n— fixed", pos="1.6,-0.6!"];

  S -> T;
  T -> R;
  R -> S;
}
----

In a school year *time is fixed* — the year ends on its date — and *resources are
fixed* — you are the team you are, with three afternoons a week. Only scope can
move.

That is why a project that is "almost finished" in June is normally a project
whose scope was never cut. And it explains the quiet failure mode: quality is not
a fourth corner of the triangle, it is what gets sacrificed when the other three
are all treated as fixed. Nobody decides to lower quality; it simply happens,
without a decision anybody could later point to.

Points the answer must contain:

* Scope, time, resources
* Time and resources are fixed by the school year and the team
* Scope is the only variable; if it is treated as fixed too, quality gives way
  silently
====

== Where does the initial situation live in the project repository, and why there?

covers: lo-2

.Answer
[%collapsible]
====
In `openspec/config.yaml`, under `context:` — unchanged, the same text that goes
into the project proposal.

The reason is the rule that runs through the whole course: *one text, not two*.
If the initial situation lives in a Word document for the supervisor and in a
paraphrase for the repository, the two drift apart within weeks, and then neither
is authoritative.

Putting it there also means the agent reads exactly what the supervisor reads. It
needs the same information and for the same reason: it is the only place that
says what the work is *for*, and without it every proposal is judged only against
what was literally asked for, never against the problem behind it.

Points the answer must contain:

* `openspec/config.yaml` under `context:`
* One text instead of two; the agent and the supervisor read the same thing
====
