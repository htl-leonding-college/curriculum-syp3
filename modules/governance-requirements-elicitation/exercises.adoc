== Extract a data model from one form

kind: drill

Here is a sign-in sheet as it hangs in a club:

----
Trainingsliste                       Gruppe: ____________
Datum: __________   Trainer/in: ______________________

Nr | Name                | Beitrag bezahlt | anwesend | Bemerkung
 1 |                     |  ja / nein      |    x     |
 2 |                     |  ja / nein      |    x     |
...
20 |                     |  ja / nein      |    x     |

Unterschrift Trainer/in: ______________________
----

Extract the candidate attributes with name, type, range and whether they are
mandatory. Then write down three questions this sheet raises.

.Solution
[%collapsible]
====
Attributes: `gruppe` (text, mandatory), `datum` (date, mandatory), `trainer`
(text, mandatory), `nummer` (integer 1–20), `name` (text), `beitrag_bezahlt`
(boolean), `anwesend` (boolean, recorded as a cross — absence is *no cross*, not
a "no"), `bemerkung` (free text, optional), `unterschrift` (signature,
mandatory).

Two structural findings come for free. The sheet caps a group at *20* — is that
the paper or the rule? And `anwesend` has no third state, so "excused" currently
lives in `Bemerkung` as free text.

Three questions worth taking to the interview:

. Is the maximum of 20 a property of groups or of the sheet?
. What does an empty `anwesend` mean — absent, or not yet recorded?
. What is written in `Bemerkung`, and does anybody read it afterwards?

Note that all three come with evidence attached. That is what makes them good
questions.
====

== Fix six interview questions

kind: drill

Mark the suggestive and the closed questions, and rewrite them:

. "You would like an app, wouldn't you?"
. "Is the paper list annoying?"
. "How many members are in a group?"
. "Do you want the export as PDF or as Excel?"
. "Should the system be fast?"
. "Is it not a problem that the list is often incomplete?"

.Solution
[%collapsible]
====
1, 2, 4 and 6 are suggestive — each offers its own answer, and 4 additionally
assumes that an export is wanted at all. 3 is closed but legitimate: a number is
exactly what is being asked for, and it is better taken from the documents
anyway. 5 is unusable because "fast" has no unit.

Rewritten:

. "Walk me through how you record attendance today."
. "What is the most annoying part of the current process?"
. (keep — or read it off the sheet and confirm it)
. "What happens to the list after training?" — the export format follows from the
  answer.
. "How long may it take before it disturbs your work?"
. "How complete is the list at the end of a week, and what follows from a gap?"

The pattern: replace your hypothesis with a question about the last concrete
occurrence.
====

== Decide which technique fits

kind: drill

Name the technique and justify it in one sentence:

. The trainers say the paper list works fine, but three of them keep a private
  Excel sheet.
. You need to know how many of the 400 members would use online booking.
. Nobody can say which fields the club's annual report needs.
. Two trainers describe the cancellation rule differently.

.Solution
[%collapsible]
====
. *Beobachtung* — description and behaviour disagree, and only watching resolves
  that; an interview returns the description again.
. *Fragebogen* — a question about quantity across a population too large to
  interview, with closed answers and no need for follow-ups.
. *Dokumentenanalyse* — last year's report exists; its fields are the answer, and
  reading it costs nobody anything.
. *Interview*, with both of them and the document in front of you — the
  contradiction is exactly what an interview is for, and the statute probably
  settles it.
====

== Write the assumption list

kind: drill

From the exercises above, write five assumptions in the form
`We assume X. Source: Y. To be confirmed by: Z.`

.Solution
[%collapsible]
====
For example:

----
We assume a group has at most 20 members.
  Source: the sign-in sheet has 20 rows. To be confirmed by: the head trainer.

We assume an empty "anwesend" means absent.
  Source: assumption, no document says so. To be confirmed by: any trainer.

We assume "excused" is not recorded separately today.
  Source: no field on the sheet, only Bemerkung. To be confirmed by: interview.
----

Two properties make such a list worth writing: each line has a *source*, so a
reader can judge how solid it is, and each names *who* can settle it, so it can
actually be closed. A list without those two columns turns into a wish list
nobody works through.
====
