== Learning outcomes

* [[lo-1]] Build a weighted scoring table from criteria, weights and ratings
* [[lo-2]] Choose criteria that discriminate between the alternatives
* [[lo-3]] Read the result correctly — including when it says the alternatives
  are equal
* [[lo-4]] Name the ways a weighted scoring can be manipulated

== The method

*Nutzwertanalyse* — weighted scoring — turns a decision between alternatives into
something you can hand to somebody else and have them follow.

. List the criteria that matter.
. Give each a weight; the weights sum to 100.
. Rate each alternative per criterion, on a fixed scale (say 0–10).
. Multiply, sum, compare.

The number is not the point. The *visible reasoning* is: anyone can now ask
"why is feasibility weighted higher than novelty?" — and that is a question the
team can answer, unlike "why did you pick A?".

== Choosing criteria

A criterion is useful only if the alternatives differ on it. "Must be software"
scores 10 for everything and contributes nothing but arithmetic.

[cols="1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Weak criterion |Better criterion

|"Is interesting"
|"At least three of us want to work on it in February"

|"Modern technology"
|"Uses at most one technology none of us has used before"

|"Useful"
|"There is a named person who will use it and can be asked questions"
|===

Set the weights *before* you rate the alternatives. Otherwise the weights become
the place where the preferred answer is engineered.

== Reading the result

* A clear gap — take the winner.
* A gap of a few percent — the method says: these are equivalent. Then decide on
  something the table does not capture, and write down what that was.
* A result that feels wrong — look for the criterion you left out. That feeling
  is usually information, not stubbornness.

== Decisions

* Every team documents its project choice as a weighted scoring table in the
  project proposal.
* Criteria and weights are the team's own. There is no prescribed catalogue —
  a borrowed catalogue produces borrowed decisions.
* Weights are fixed before the ratings are entered.
* If two alternatives end within a few percent of each other, the team writes
  one sentence saying what actually decided it.

== Pitfalls

* Tuning weights until the desired alternative wins. It happens, it is visible in
  the history, and it destroys the point of the exercise.
* Criteria that do not discriminate. They add work and change nothing.
* Too many criteria. Above roughly seven, each weight is so small that the sums
  are noise.
* Treating the number as the decision. The table supports a decision; the team
  makes it.
