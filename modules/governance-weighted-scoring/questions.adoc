== Describe the four steps of a weighted scoring

covers: lo-1

Expected answer points:

* Collect criteria, assign weights summing to 100, rate alternatives on a fixed
  scale, multiply and sum
* The comparison is between the weighted sums

== What makes a criterion useful, and what makes one useless?

covers: lo-2

Expected answer points:

* Useful when the alternatives actually differ on it
* Useless when everything scores the same — it only adds arithmetic
* Vague criteria ("interesting") should be replaced by observable ones

== Two alternatives end 745 to 738. What does that mean?

covers: lo-3

Expected answer points:

* The method says they are equivalent — the gap is within the noise
* Decide on something the table does not capture and write down what it was
* Do not pretend the number decided it

== How can a weighted scoring be manipulated, and what prevents it?

covers: lo-4

Expected answer points:

* Adjusting weights after seeing the ratings
* Adding criteria that favour the preferred option
* Prevention: fix weights before rating, keep the table in version control where
  changes are visible

== Why is the result written into the project proposal at all?

covers: lo-1, lo-3

Expected answer points:

* It makes the choice traceable for someone who was not in the room
* The reasoning, not the number, is what can be questioned and defended
